KMDigitizer v1.0.0

Reconstruct Individual Patient Data from Published Kaplan-Meier Curves

The Guyot Algorithm (2012)

  1. Digitized KM coordinates provide S(t) at discrete time points
  2. Between consecutive time points, estimate the number of events and censorings using the at-risk table (if available) or by assuming uniform censoring
  3. For each interval [t_j, t_{j+1}]: compute d_j (events) and c_j (censored) from the survival drop and at-risk numbers
  4. Generate individual-level records: d_j patients with event at t_j, c_j patients censored uniformly in the interval

Output

Reconstructed IPD with columns: time, event (1=event, 0=censored), arm. This IPD can be used for:

  • IPD meta-analysis (two-stage or one-stage)
  • RMST computation (restricted mean survival time)
  • Flexible parametric modeling (Royston-Parmar)
  • Subgroup-level survival analysis

Accuracy

Guyot et al. (2012) showed that reconstructed IPD produces hazard ratios within 1-3% of the original trial data for most scenarios. Accuracy improves with more digitized points and availability of at-risk tables.

Limitations

  • Assumes piecewise-constant hazard within each interval
  • Without at-risk table, censoring distribution is assumed uniform
  • Cannot recover covariates — only marginal survival per arm
  • Digitization errors propagate to IPD (use fine-grained coordinates)

References

  • Guyot P et al. Enhanced secondary analysis of survival data: reconstructing the data from published KM survival curves. BMC Med Res Methodol. 2012;12:9.
  • Wei Y, Royston P. Reconstructing time-to-event data from published KM curves. Stata J. 2017;17:786-802.
